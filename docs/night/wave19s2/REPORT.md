# Wave 19 S2: sediment volume, sectional cost, representation diagnostics

## Sediment mass and volume

The independent SI calculation is `0.1 mol/L ? 0.001 L ? 0.14332 kg/mol = 1.4332e-5 kg` AgCl per normalized unit, or 14.332 mg. At 5560 kg/m3 its compact volume is `2.577697841726619e-9 m3 = 2.57770 mm3`. `STAGE3_AGCL_SOLID_M3_PER_UNIT` agrees to better than 1e-9 relative in the audit test. `AGCL_SOLID_M3_PER_UNIT` is the legacy 0.01 M value, one tenth as large; the stage 3 field passes the 0.1 M value to `SilverSedimentField`. Its `values` are normalized masses, `bulkVolumeM3(mass)` is in m3, `equivalentFillHeight` is in metres, and each height texture texel is `bulkVolumeM3(column mass) / (4r? / (nx nz))` in metres. The latter is a *column* height above the hemispherical floor, not a level fill or a uniform coating. The independent test checks double-precision bulk volume and bowl conversion to 1e-9 relative; the stored Float32 height texel agrees to 1e-7 relative, consistent with its precision.

At 20 s LOW, `0.006414` unit is `0.0919254 mg`, compact volume `0.0165334 mm3`, and, at 90% porosity, bulk volume `0.165334 mm3`. The uniform cross-section thickness at the actual 7 mm **radius** is `0.00107403 mm`. Rounding the mass to 0.09 mg gives the customer's `0.0162 / 0.162 mm3` and about `0.00105 mm`. At porosity 0.894 rounded to three decimals, bulk volume is `0.155975 mm3`, uniform thickness `0.00101323 mm`, and rounded-bowl level fill `0.08439 mm`. The replay file gives the more precise 20 s LOW state `0.0064136648225` unit and porosity `0.8935912562`, with bowl fill `0.0842227 mm` and maximum height texel `0.00385818 mm`. At exactly 90% porosity the bowl fill is `0.08689 mm`. The code's `heightFromVolume` uses `V(h)=?h?(r-h/3)` below 7 mm; a small volume therefore has a much larger *level height* at the narrow bottom than its cross-section thickness. These are distinct physical definitions, not a mass discrepancy.

The wave18 report's `0.167 mm` is the **full** 14.332 mg deposit's uniform thickness at 90% porosity: `25.77698 mm3 / 153.938 mm2 = 0.16745 mm`. It is not the 20 s LOW thickness. That report correctly identifies the 20 s bowl fill as about 0.084 mm and the maximum height texel as 0.0039 mm. The maximum texel can be below the equivalent level fill because it represents a distributed, locally thin surface layer. No reported number requires scaling the sediment mass. The old no-texture mesh fallback did have a plain geometry error: it used `silverSedimentThickness`, a dimensionless visual law, for a physical height. The fallback now uses conserved bulk volume over the actual grid-column area; the normal runtime path already supplies the physical texture. The mesh interpolates and tapers these heights for display, so its triangulated volume is not used as a mass ledger.

[`volume-table.json`](volume-table.json) tabulates 0.01%, 0.1%, 1%, 10%, and 100% of one unit at porosities 0.90, 0.80, 0.70, and 0.55. It distinguishes: (i) bulk volume divided by `?r?`; (ii) a coating on the **inside** hemispherical surface, `2?(r?-(r-t)?)/3`, plus the requested outer-shell alternative `2?((r+t)?-r?)/3`; (iii) a level bowl fill from `tubeVessel.heightFromVolume`; and (iv) a deliberately concentrated one-column upper bound on the 16?16 grid. The last is not a prediction of an angle-of-repose mound. The model's actual maximum column height depends on deposition and `settleCurd`; the current HIGH dumps give 0.0000267, 0.0000768, 0.000343, 0.002731, and 0.017386 mm at 3, 6, 9, 14, and 22 s. The renderer uses that height texture on the real rounded floor. A hypothetical single-column height in the table can exceed the vessel height and is only an algebraic concentration bound.

## Sectional performance and regression

`SectionalPopulation` has 13 pivots and at most 91 unordered collision pairs per wet cell. The baseline scanned the same occupied classes and held fixed mass/delta arrays, but recalculated the Brownian and shear pair factors, product pivot search, and pivot weight inside every event. Transport computed x/y/z coordinates for each occupied section cell. The optimized class caches pair factors and pivot weights once per population and voxel coordinates once per grid. It changes no collision cap, section scheme, slip, transfer coefficient, skip threshold, or texture cadence. Zero additional JS objects are allocated inside `collide` or `transport` per step by this change; the field as a whole still allocates elsewhere. The cache adds about 3 bytes per voxel plus 4.2 KB for 169 pair slots. The existing mass plus delta arrays use 0.719 MB LOW and 1.704 MB HIGH; aggregate and sediment texture CPU arrays use 55,872 and 132,096 bytes.

The retained [`sectionalPopulationBaseline.ts`](../../../tests/night/wave19s2/sectionalPopulationBaseline.ts) is the pre-optimization implementation. A deterministic real-pour replay compared every 50 ms row for 200 steps on both grids, including all 13 section totals, fine, nodes, sediment and extent. It passed `max absolute difference <1e-15` and `max relative difference <1e-12` for entries at least `1e-12`; this tolerance permits floating-point reassociation in the precomputed shear factor. No threshold mass was skipped. Both variants passed the field's every-step `1e-12` mass invariant.

The final [`benchmark.json`](benchmark.json) is 20 warm-up plus 200 measured real-pour steps per variant, measured in one Vitest process. Times include host contention and instrumentation overhead. The first standalone run was slower overall, so these are comparative measurements, not a frame-rate guarantee.

| Grid / variant | Whole step p50 / p95 / peak ms | Collision p50 / p95 ms | Transport p50 / p95 ms |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| LOW baseline | 14.36 / 19.51 / 122.53 | 8.79 / 13.39 | 1.56 / 2.74 |
| LOW optimized | 8.32 / 10.70 / 73.50 | 3.05 / 4.15 | 1.47 / 1.97 |
| HIGH baseline | 31.29 / 36.15 / 44.67 | 19.53 / 22.92 | 3.34 / 4.35 |
| HIGH optimized | 19.28 / 23.36 / 40.94 | 7.43 / 9.59 | 3.38 / 4.53 |

Measured active cells at the end were 1,068 LOW and 2,448 HIGH. The residual step time includes reaction, fine transport, promotion checks, section rasterization, texture updates and other field work; no per-function claim is made for those components. Peak process heap was 29.6–32.8 MB across runs. Sampled heap deltas swung positive and negative under GC; they cannot be interpreted as bytes or object allocations per step. The zero-allocation statement above applies only to explicit allocations in the two sectional hot loops. Deferring texture refresh would change frozen visual states, so it was not used.

## Synchronized representation diagnostics

The wave18 HIGH dumps in `docs/night/wave16shared/wave18_state_{3,6,9,14,22}s.json` supplied the actual deterministic population. Their section totals equal the aggregate ledger within 1e-12, and all have **zero visible nodes**. Thus, for these states, the aggregate RGBA texture is entirely the unresolved section population and the nodes-only panel is correctly empty. The [`sheet_3s.png`](sheet_3s.png), [`sheet_6s.png`](sheet_6s.png), [`sheet_9s.png`](sheet_9s.png), [`sheet_14s.png`](sheet_14s.png), and [`sheet_22s.png`](sheet_22s.png) CPU sheets show columns fine, sections, nodes, sediment, combined and rows the four optics modes. Each row uses the same frozen dump and the existing optical coefficients. A replay assertion compares the CPU fine conversion against the live stage-3 `pixels` texture to 1e-7 relative; stage 3 overwrites the legacy optical-mix calculation with raw suspension density. The CPU sheets use a fixed gray background for physical modes and black for diagnostic modes. PPM originals are alongside them. [`optical-metrics.json`](optical-metrics.json) records dense-core luminance and full-panel contrast; [`optical-depth.json`](optical-depth.json) records straight occupied ray estimates without grain, interpolation or alpha stopping. At 9 s the section optical-depth p50/p95 is 3.30/36.97, while the fine p50/p95 is 2.19/8.85. At 22 s the section p50/p95 is 1.45/10.81. Dense-core luminance in the 9 s gray-background CPU sheet is 0.659 for fine, 0.652 for sections and 0.674 combined; section full-panel contrast is 0.498, mostly spanning gray background to saturated white. These are CPU estimates, not browser pixels or reference-video measurements. The CPU sediment-only and combined ray samples mostly miss the micron-scale mesh, so their blank sediment panel is a sampling limitation, not missing deposited mass.

Classification from the mass dumps and sheets: **A**, microscopic particles exist in sections 0–5 and carry almost all aggregate mass. **B**, their voxel concentrations do form contiguous mesoscopic bright regions with local clear space; the sections-only view is visibly nonempty while nodes are absent. **C**, no individually settling millimetre flocs exist in these five states. **D**, unresolved populations can create connected white cloud regions without inventing solid, but these small CPU sheets show broad smooth lobes and heavy saturation rather than convincing irregular hanging cotton. The actual shader, camera, lab background and the reference must be inspected before judging a visual match. Extinction and alpha were not raised.

`tests/_wave19/captureRepresentations.mjs` is the browser matrix for five representations, four optics modes and lab/black/gray backgrounds at the same five paused states, with identical camera and coefficients. It uses the full aggregate texture as sections only when the node count is zero; if nodes appear, it requires S1's `field.aggregateSectionTexture` as an RGBA texture with the same voxel layout, and subtracts its mass-weighted voxel channels to form a nodes-only diagnostic texture. `tests/_wave19/runComparison.mjs` runs that matrix, a continuous 24 s real-game recording and the existing stage-matched reference comparison. Neither browser script was run here: this worker has no browser or accessible reference video. The browser capture can reveal shader link, sorting, section shape, mesh aliasing, and video contrast results that the CPU diagnostics cannot establish.
