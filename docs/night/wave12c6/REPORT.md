# Wave 12 C6: conservative subgrid reagent exchange

## Result and decision

The gated experiment increases AgCl formed in the real stepped-delivery replay, from 0.25764 to 0.27696 at 9 s and from 0.32457 to 0.34534 at 14 s. Both reagents and the product conserve to better than 1e-12 at every 50 ms step, and no negative or nonfinite concentrations were measured. The fine field remains one connected region at both 10% and 50% thresholds. Its normalized total variation falls from 0.9299 to 0.9122 at 9 s and from 0.8608 to 0.8428 at 14 s, however, and its 10% surface-to-volume ratio also falls slightly. This configuration **does not meet the spatial-contrast acceptance condition**. Keep `__silverSubgrid` off by default. It is a conservative experimental option, not a validated production setting or a visual fix.

No renderer, liquid solver, delivery ledger, precipitation law, or fine-suspension transport constant changed. The C5 jet implementation is absent from this worktree, so B (jet only) and D (jet plus C6) are pending coordinator integration. The replay accepts `__silverJetModel` and can generate B/D with `WAVE12_COMBINED=1` once C5 is present. A/C data here were generated with that flag false.

## Physical question and real-game evidence

The supplied real-game dumps show 1.0 delivered chloride amount at 1.5 s, with 0.92856 dissolved chloride, 1.07856 dissolved silver, and 0.07144 AgCl. At 9 s, 0.76672 chloride and 0.91672 silver remain, with 0.23328 AgCl. The very small simultaneous dissolved overlap (0.001158 amount at 9 s) **cannot by itself diagnose mixing**: fast precipitation removes an overlap shortly after contact. The transport and source rates are the relevant comparison. The diagnostic replay starts at first field delivery, about 600 ms later than the dump click clock, and uses the same 0.02 mL stepped ledger pattern, two parcels per 50 ms step, 1.0 mL initial liquid and the 6.5 mm impact. Do not equate its early clock to the dump clock or impose an extent from the reference video.

For each wet positive-direction face the CSV records the absolute Cl fraction jump, weighted by the mean Ag fraction and face area. Summing gives `interfaceAreaM2 = Σ |Cl_i-Cl_j|/max(Cl) × (Ag_i+Ag_j)/(2 max(Ag)) × A_face`. The separate `isoAreaM2` sums wet face areas across the 5%-of-maximum Cl threshold; this is a voxel 3D surface proxy. Both are measured *after* the step's reaction. `agAdvFluxPerS` and `clAdvFluxPerS` sum directional upwind advective fluxes into the opposite side of those threshold faces. `agBaseFluxPerS` and `clBaseFluxPerS` use the solver's effective 2e-6 m²/s scalar transport plus its 0.3 eddy scale and 0.12-per-step face cap. `agModelFluxPerS` and `clModelFluxPerS` estimate the added C6 flux there. All are normalized amount per second. These are gross instantaneous interface flux estimates, not an exact integrated flux ledger; they may double-count paths through multiple faces. The source is the actual `productSource` sum divided by 0.05 s.

The requested source / limiting-species incoming-flux quotient is in `contactFractionProxy`. It is 7.47 at 0.5 s, 3.63 at 9 s and 4.37 at 14 s in A. Values above one expose the limit of treating a *post-reaction, thresholded, instantaneous gross-face estimate* as the whole contact flux; the source also consumes material already in interface cells, and the boundary moves. It is **not** a physical consumption probability. A bounded local consumption measure, source divided by the pre-reaction co-located limiting pair in the marked interface cells, is 0.926 at 0.5 s, 0.419 at 9 s and 0.324 at 14 s. The rate law can react over 80% of a fresh equal-concentration pair in one step, as wave 11 C3 established. Reservoir amounts remain large while contact-area and local supply proxies are small and source declines from about .049/s to .013/s. Together with the C6 response, this supports a **transport/contact limit rather than a global chemistry-rate limit**. The exact physical contribution of molecular transport versus unresolved jet entrainment remains uncalibrated.

The CSVs give every 50 ms through 14 s: incoming chloride (`delivered`), dissolved Ag and Cl, cumulative AgCl (`extent`), the two interface areas, four resolved transport-flux estimates, two model flux estimates, source and contact quotients, and variance for Ag, Cl and fine suspension. The larger absolute variance partly reflects more product, so it does not rescue the normalized contrast loss.

## Model

After the existing solver transports the two ions and incoming tracer, and before local precipitation, C6 measures the symmetric strain tensor from centered or one-sided wet-cell velocity differences. For each wet face it sets a **modelled unresolved-mixing coefficient** `D_sgs = min(3e-6, 0.25 Δ² |S|) m²/s`, with `Δ` the cube root of cell volume. Options can set `coefficient` within [0,1] and `maximum` within [0,1e-5]. The 0.25 multiplier is an exploratory closure strength, not a measured Smagorinsky calibration. The upper bound limits grid-scale exchange; the additional per-face amount fraction is capped at 0.08 per 50 ms. It is zero for still liquid. These model coefficients are distinct from molecular diffusion (Cl− about 1.9e-9 m²/s, Ag+ about 1.7e-9 m²/s) and from the pre-existing solver's **effective** 2e-6 m²/s mixing.

For each scalar, a face proposes `min(0.08, D_sgs dt/h²) × (q_i-q_j)`. The sum of outgoing proposals from a donor is scaled to at most its available amount; the signed transfer is subtracted from one cell and added to its neighbor. This is a conservative positive face exchange, applied independently to Ag+, Cl− and the tracer. It changes cell means only by those paired transfers. The existing `precipitatePair` then consumes equal available Ag+ and Cl− amounts per cell and puts all new AgCl into `suspension[0]`. Its rate and local equilibrium rule are unchanged. C1 fine transport remains 1e-9 m²/s and eddy scale 0.02; C6 never exchanges fine solid. With the flag absent or false, the new branch is skipped and the baseline golden extents are bit-identical in the replay.
