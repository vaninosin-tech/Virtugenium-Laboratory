## Customer decision (binding)
The fine suspension gives convincing milkiness and stays untouched. What is missing is what the reference shows: large, dense, irregular cotton-like aggregates, connected and hanging white formations, clearly distinguishable fluffy structures, clear spaces between them, and a gradually visible curdy deposit. Render physically represented larger aggregates as such formations, using the material and geometry interfaces from F3. Do not alter chemical production, the fluid transport or the pouring; do not create white geometry independently of AgCl mass; do not raise global alpha to hide an inadequate aggregate field; do not duplicate aggregate mass in the fine-suspension optical field; keep `__silverJetModel` and `__silverSubgrid` OFF; keep the no-aggregation baseline selectable (`window.__silverAgg`); experiments 1, 3, 4 render identically.

## Data interface from F3 (additive; existing names kept)
`field.texture`/`fineTexture` (fine suspension), `field.aggregateTexture` (`Data3DTexture`, RGBA float, same grid as `texture`: R aggregate density (AgCl mass of all aggregate representations to be drawn as aggregates, in the texture convention mass times `activeCount`), G looseness 0 compact to 1 open, B compaction, A orientation/elongation), `field.denseTexture`, `field.sedimentHeightTexture` (RedFormat float, nx by nz, deposit height in metres per column), `field.flocSystem.nodes` (x, y, z, mass, radius, links), and possibly `field.aggregateSectionTexture` and `field.visibleFlocs` summaries ({center, radius of gyration, mass, node ids}) which F3 documents in `docs/night/wave18f3/REPORT.md`. Use `field.aggregateTexture ?? field.denseTexture`, read the optional ones if present.

## A. Visible white formations
Visible aggregates should have dense white centres, irregular connected lobes, soft but ragged boundaries, hanging and curved extensions, smaller connected or partly separated fragments, uneven optical density, recognisable three-dimensional thickness and clearer spaces between concentrated structures. They must not look like transparent smoke, smooth featureless swirls, independent spherical blobs, sand or snow, flat sprites or identical cotton objects. The structure must come from the actual aggregate distribution: the aggregate density field and the floc node list (positions, radii, masses, links). Measure first, with the CPU reference and F3's dumps, why the previous aggregates were too translucent and smooth (the p95 to p50 of the aggregate density, the number of cells over which a node's mass is spread, the gradient statistics, the optical depth distribution along rays, the compositing order) and report. If the coarse texture cannot carry the structure, add a sub-cell reconstruction in the shader from a compact per-frame uniform or small texture of the visible flocs (a limited number of nodes near each sample: for example a capped list of the largest floc nodes uploaded as a small data texture each time it changes (the node count is at most a few hundred; do not allocate per frame), evaluated as mass-conserving smooth kernels with link-aware elongation, with at most a few kernel evaluations per ray sample using a coarse spatial index (cell lists in a small texture)), so large flocs show connected lobes and hanging extensions at sub-cell scale; the total mass drawn must equal the node mass (test it: the integral of the reconstructed density over the domain equals the node mass within tolerance, and nodes are not drawn twice through the texture AND the kernels: define clearly which representation each unit of mass is drawn from, and remove the double use).
A dense interior may be bright white and nearly opaque; edges irregular and partly translucent; do not make all aggregates uniformly opaque; use contrast shaping that is a function of the physical density only (optical depth proportional to density to a power set by looseness, self-shadowing taps toward the light, soft rim, multiple scattering that saturates dense cores to white); procedural noise is allowed only as small-scale detail below the floc size and scaled by looseness; gaps appear only where the simulated concentration is low (no cutout masks, no black holes drawn into clouds). Keep separate debug modes: `uSolidMode` 0 combined (default), 1 fine only, 2 aggregates only, 3 sediment only, plus the four `uOpticsMode` renderings. Report added texture taps and instruction count; keep the cost near the baseline (44 median FPS and 33 FPS at the 10th percentile during the pour).

## B. Deposit
Use the actual deposited mass, the physical density and the documented packing and porosity model from F3 (`sedimentHeightTexture`). The sediment gradually builds a soft irregular layer following the real rounded bottom of the vessel (`tubeVessel` bowl). Do not invent a height multiplier; a very thin deposit is acceptable if the modeled mass is genuinely small: if the budget predicts a nearly invisible deposit, say so with the numbers instead of fabricating solid (the 0.1 M budget is 14.3 mg of AgCl, compact volume 2.6e-9 m3, bulk about 0.026 mL at 90 percent porosity, about 0.7 mm uniform fill in the 7 mm tube).

## C. Verify the moire is really gone (not hidden by thinness)
Do not claim the moire is fixed because the deposit is too thin to see. Build a separate diagnostic path with an explicitly labeled increased display scale or a controlled synthetic deposited-mass field (a dev-only switch, for example `window.__silverSedimentDiagnosticScale` and a synthetic height map option in the capture scripts and in the CPU reference; it must be inert by default and never alter the simulation state) that renders the deposit at 5 to 20 times its physical height or from a synthetic mass field so the surface shader can be judged, and measure the regular-pattern energy (2D FFT peak of the high-pass crop relative to broadband energy) before (commit `7690e47` shader) and after. Find and fix any remaining lattice-aligned frequency (detail frequencies versus the height texture pitch and the vertex pitch, interpolation of the height map, repeating UVs, z-fighting with the glass or the volume mesh, transparency sorting, aliasing below two pixels), without blurring the whole deposit. Provide before and after crops (CPU reference and the coordinator's browser captures) and a static scan test.

## D. Fine suspension plus aggregates plus sediment
Fine turbidity (diffuse white), larger loose aggregates and the deposit must be visible together in the combined mode; the same state is rendered in fine only, aggregates only, combined and sediment only (identical coefficients, identical camera); labelled PNG captures for black, neutral gray and laboratory backgrounds; luminance contrast numbers (dense floc core versus the bright lab wall). The lab-background contrast must show the aggregates as distinctly whiter than the fine milk and the wall.
