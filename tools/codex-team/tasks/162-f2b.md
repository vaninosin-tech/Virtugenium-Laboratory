## A. Why are the aggregates too translucent? (measure first, with the CPU reference and F1b's dumps)
Quantify with numbers which of these dominates: insufficient aggregate optical density (extinction coefficient per unit aggregate density), an excessively diffuse mass distribution (the aggregate density texture is smooth: its p95 to p50 ratio, its gradient statistics, the fraction of aggregate mass in cells above an optical-significance threshold), incorrect scattering strength, excessive opacity blending (compositing order of the fine layer and the aggregate layer, early exit), poor volume sampling (88 steps through a 16 x 32 x 16 grid: field variation per step, linear filtering of narrow features, jitter), or inadequate contrast between dense and sparse regions (the looseness channel mapping). Note that a large aggregate can look optically dense because its mass is concentrated in a smaller, more structured region (the field's rasterisation spreads each node's mass over cells: check how many cells a node's mass is spread over and whether that spreading itself washes the structure out; report it and, if so, request or implement (inside your files) a sharper sampling such as a sub-cell reconstruction from the node list (`field.flocSystem.nodes` positions, radii and masses are public) evaluated in the shader as a small number of metaballs/kernels around the nodes near each sample, with mass-conserving kernels, instead of only reading the coarse texture; decide by measurement). Do not make an aggregate uniformly opaque with an arbitrary high alpha; do not create mass in the shader (total optical use of each state's texture stays tied to its mass).

## B. Dense wet-cotton structure from the simulated state
The aggregates should have dense white interiors, uneven local density, irregular connected lobes, ragged and partly translucent edges, hanging extensions, smaller partly separated formations, variation in shape and thickness and recognisable depth in the liquid. The large structure must come from the simulated aggregate field (and the node list); procedural noise adds only small-scale optical detail below the aggregate size, scaled by looseness and orientation. Do not use sprites, meshes of cotton, sand grains, crystalline snowflakes, identical blobs, rigid chunks, a cutout mask to manufacture gaps or black holes unrelated to the simulation: clear spaces must appear where the simulated concentration is low. Use contrast shaping that is a function of the physical density only (for example an optical depth proportional to density to a power set by the looseness channel, self-shadowing taps toward the light, a soft rim, a multiple-scattering term that saturates dense cores to white) and keep the fine suspension rendering simultaneously (never replace it, never double count). Keep separate modes `uSolidMode`: 0 combined (default), 1 fine only, 2 aggregates only, 3 sediment only, and the four `uOpticsMode` renderings of the same state. Report added texture taps and instructions and keep the cost near the baseline (44 median FPS and 33 FPS at the 10th percentile during the pour in the real browser; no extra full-volume pass).
