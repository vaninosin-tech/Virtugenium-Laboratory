## B. Momentum penetration into a shallow liquid of changing depth
Measure the liquid depth at every simulation step (`level` minus the bowl bottom along the jet axis; the depth grows from about 7 mm to 14 mm and more as 1 mL is added; note the effective radius). Replace the fixed default penetration by a model that depends on the instantaneous incoming momentum, the liquid surface position, the existing fluid velocity, the distance to the bottom, the side walls and the entrainment, with no jet forcing below the vessel bottom or in nonexistent fluid. Do NOT forbid the jet from reaching the bottom: a real stream can reach the bottom of a shallow layer; when it does, the remaining momentum must produce a physically consistent response (stagnation and a radial wall jet along the bottom, deflection upward along the walls, recirculation), by letting the solver respond to a source that is applied only in existing wet cells and whose remainder is not carried through the bottom (for example by treating the bottom as a wall for the jet force with the pressure projection supplying the reaction), not by discarding or smearing the momentum over the whole volume. Account for the decay of the jet momentum with depth by entrainment (the textbook round-jet law with a stated entrainment coefficient and a stated virtual origin), and for momentum dissipation. Keep the source localised near the jet axis and its entrainment cone; surrounding fluid must respond through the pressure projection and the existing flow solver; do not distribute the impulse over the full receiving volume.
