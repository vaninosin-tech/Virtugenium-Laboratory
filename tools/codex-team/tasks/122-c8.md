## What to build
1. **A simulation-state recorder usable in the real browser and offline** (`tests/_wave13/recordRun.mjs` plus a pure analysis library `tests/_wave13/analysis.mjs` unit-tested in `tests/night/wave13c8/`): during a real-game pour (as `tests/_trace.mjs` and `tests/_wave11/browserCommon.mjs` do: real `runStandAction`, stage 3, aggregation off, precipitation on, the simulation clock of the app and `window.__diag` for pause/resume) record on a fixed simulation-time grid (every 50 ms from the click to 14 s, plus dumps of the full fields at 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4.5, 6, 9, 12, 14 s after the click): incoming stream and actual impact location (ledger event point, `window.__pourStreamDebug`), the current liquid depth along the axis (field `liquidLevel` and the mask), local downward liquid velocity along the jet axis and radial or lateral displacement (read the solver's face velocities: find the public accessors in `lib/tubeFlowSolver.ts` and the field `velocity`; if a quantity cannot be read from the app without editing the solver, say exactly which accessor you need as an integration request), fluid movement near the bottom (radial speed in the lowest two rows), recirculation (sign changes of the vertical velocity across the central plane, circulation integral), the passive tracer field (run variant with `window.__silverDiag = "tracer"`), dissolved Ag+ and Cl- fields, the local precipitation source, the fine-suspension field, the solver step times (`solver.lastStepMs`) and the reagent-transport time if exposed. Separate the five processes in the analysis, with their own measures: (1) incoming liquid penetration (depth of the tracer front and of the downward velocity), (2) hydrodynamic entrainment (volume flux into the jet column across a control surface, tracer dilution), (3) dissolved-reactant transport (Ag+ and Cl- fluxes through their interface, interface area), (4) rapid local precipitation (source rate, consumption fraction at contact), (5) transport of the already formed AgCl (advection of suspension centroid and moments, independent of the source).
2. **Spatial-structure metrics of the fine suspension over time** (library functions with tests on synthetic fields: smooth blob, two blobs, a filament, uniform, a thin sheet): concentration variance and coefficient of variation among occupied cells, gradient magnitude statistics, occupied volume fraction at thresholds, connected-region count and sizes (26-connectivity) at 10 and 50 percent of p95, S/V of the iso-regions, vertical and radial concentration profiles, the Gini or entropy of the concentration distribution, and a scale-free measure of how uniform the field is (for example the ratio of the 90th to the 10th percentile among occupied cells). The metrics compare the spatial development, not only the total mass; a field that fills the tube uniformly by 3 s must score as uniform.
3. **Optical comparison on identical frozen states** (`tests/_wave13/captureOpticsMatrix13.mjs`, a copy of `tests/_wave11/captureOpticsMatrix.mjs` made configurable by the `INIT` environment variable and by a list of timestamps, with the filenames carrying the configuration name): black, neutral gray and normal laboratory backgrounds, the four optics modes, identical optical coefficients (the script records them), no opacity change per background or per configuration. Add a numeric optical-contrast report from the saved PNGs (mean, p10 and p90 luminance of the liquid region, the fraction of liquid pixels brighter than the background plus a threshold, the horizontal and vertical luminance variance inside the liquid region) so uniform milky fills versus structured clouds can be told apart without looking.
4. **Performance during the pour** (`tests/_wave13/measureFpsPour.mjs`): real-time (no `SLOW` dilation), measure frame times from 1 s before the NaCl click to 8 s after it (the window that includes the delivery, the jet and the forming cloud), repeated for N runs per configuration (at least 3 runs per configuration, configurations given by `INIT`): median FPS, 10th-percentile FPS, peak frame time, number of frames above 50 ms and 100 ms, the per-step fluid-solver time series (`solver.lastStepMs` sampled each frame by an in-page hook), the reagent/species transport time if the field exposes it (otherwise the difference between the field advance time measured around `field.advance` through a dev-only wrapper installed in the page before the pour and the solver time), CPU and, when `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2` is available, GPU time of the frame with the cloud on and off. Report the peak simulation workload during pouring, not after it, and whether the extra solver cost of the flags is justified by the structure measures. Keep the capture itself light (do not draw overlays, do not read pixels during measurement).
5. **Regression harness** (`tests/_wave13/regressionAB.mjs`): a browser script that reruns Diagnostics A and B on the actual game for a given configuration and asserts: the stream falls from above and the first valid impact is at the real receiving surface; the delivered volume equals the ledger total and the receiving tube volume increases by exactly the delivered amount; the source container volume decreased by the requested amount; no tracer, Cl- or product before the first valid impact; no duplicate events (event ids unique); Cl- first appears within 3 mm of the impact point and within the top rows; the first product source cells lie where both Ag+ and Cl- are present; `Ag+ + AgCl` equals the initial silver and `Cl- + AgCl` equals the delivered chloride to 1e-9 at every recorded step; no precipitation without an overlap; the simulation clock timing (first impact near 600 ms after the click, the first Cl- cell within 50 ms of it). Write the pure checks as a function over the recorded trace so they are unit-tested on a synthetic trace and also runnable offline on `trace.json` files produced by `tests/_trace.mjs` (the coordinator keeps real-game traces from the earlier waves in `D:/Claude/virtugenium-night/frames/*/trace.json`; use their structure as the format reference: `kind`, `sim`, `A`, `B`, `C`, `slice` records).
