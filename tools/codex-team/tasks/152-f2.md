## State of play (customer decision, binding)
The fine suspension (milky volumetric scattering, already working in the real browser) is the ACCEPTED foundation: do not replace it and do not change the chemistry, the transport or the fluid solver to compensate for weak rendering. Add the visual representation of the larger aggregates and of the bottom sediment from the actual state that F1 provides. The final image must contain BOTH diffuse turbidity from the fine suspension and larger loose aggregates; the same AgCl mass must not be rendered twice as independent solid material (the field keeps each unit of mass in exactly one of the three states: the shader samples each state's own texture; do not add extra opacity "to look whiter"). Experiments 1, 3, 4 render identically (this material is only used by experiment 2).

## Required appearance (reference: dense white cotton-like formations, irregular connected lobes, hanging and branching aggregates, ragged boundaries, partly detached fluffy pieces, clear space between dense regions, soft white curdy sediment)
Aggregates should look like wet, loose, irregular white material suspended underwater: dense white regions, connected irregular lobes, ragged boundaries, hanging extensions, smaller detached formations, variable opacity, soft edges, visible three-dimensional depth (scattering and self-shadowing, not a flat overlay). The LARGE structure (which lobes exist, where they hang, where gaps are) must come from the simulated aggregate field (texture R); procedural noise may only add small-scale optical detail (fibres, ragged rims at a scale below the aggregate size, never creating lobes of its own), scaled by the looseness channel (G) and orientation (A) that F1 provides so loose open aggregates are feathery and compact ones denser. Avoid sand-like grains, sharp crystalline snowflakes, perfect spheres, identical repeated cotton objects, rigid chunks and flat white overlays. Keep the existing `cotton()` idea only if its structure is driven by the field; the previous version carried the structure in procedural noise, which the customer rejected.

## Interface from F1 (additive, as the override file states)
Existing and kept: `field.texture` (fine suspension; `field.fineTexture ?? field.texture`), `field.denseTexture` (RGBA float: R rasterised aggregate density, GBA cell coordinates), `field.flocVisual`, `field.flocSystem.nodes`, `field.sedimentField`. NEW from F1: `field.aggregateTexture` (`THREE.Data3DTexture`, RGBA FloatType, same grid and normalised mapping as `texture`: R aggregate density in the convention mass times `activeCount`, G looseness 0 compact to 1 open, B mean compaction 0..1, A orientation or elongation measure 0..1 or 0) and `field.sedimentHeightTexture` (`THREE.DataTexture`, RedFormat FloatType, nx by nz, the deposit height in metres per column from the bottom of the bowl). Use `field.aggregateTexture ?? field.denseTexture` so your work degrades gracefully while F1 is unfinished. Bind in `tubeReactionVisuals.ts` (silver hunks only, smallest possible; the runtime-owned field is obtained through `runtimeSilverField` as today).

## Work
1. **Shader**: render the aggregates in the existing single ray march (`tubeSilverVolumeMaterial.ts`), keeping the fine cloud path and its `glf()` literal discipline, the four `uOpticsMode` renderings (combined, raw concentration, extinction only, scattering only) for the SAME state and adding the modes needed for the deliverables: `uSolidMode` selecting fine only, aggregates only, combined (default), and sediment only. Use the aggregate density for extinction (a looser, more porous material scatters and is translucent at low density and saturates to white at high density), single plus cheap multiple scattering with the same light as the fine cloud, a soft rim, self-shadowing taps from the density texture (a few taps, uniform loop bounds: the shader once needed minutes to compile with unrolled noise), early exit when saturated, no per-pixel `for` with non-uniform bounds beyond what exists. Report the added texture taps and instruction count and an estimate of per-pixel cost; keep the performance baseline (44 median FPS and 33 at the 10th percentile during the pour in the real browser) in mind: no extra pass, no second full-volume march.
2. **Sediment**: render the deposit as a soft, irregular, curdy white surface built from `sedimentHeightTexture` (extend `SilverSedimentGeometry` or replace its update path; you own the `SilverSedimentGeometry` class in `lib/tubeSilverSediment.ts`, F1 owns `SilverSedimentField` in the same file: keep your edits inside your class and its geometry builder to avoid merge conflicts), conforming to the rounded bottom of the real vessel (`tubeVessel` hemispherical bowl of 7 mm radius), growing progressively with deposited material, with curd-like detail from the height field and a small-scale normal perturbation, white with soft translucency at thin spots; never a flat white disk, never a fixed height, never visible before material has arrived.
