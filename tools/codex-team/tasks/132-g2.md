## 2. Q, A, U and momentum flux must be mutually consistent (check units in tests)
`Q = A_jet * U_jet` and `F = rho * Q * U_jet` (rho 1000 kg/m3). Derive Q from the delivered volume of the events (the integral of Q must equal the ledger total to 1e-9 and must not be smoothed to hide anything), U at the lip from Q and the outlet area (the stream radius that the real stream mesh draws at the lip, `PourStream` ring radius `(0.00115 - 0.0004 t) * (0.42 + 0.58 flow)`; decide and document which ring is the outlet), U at the impact by energy conservation along the fall `U_i = sqrt(U_lip^2 + 2 g h)` with `h` the real height between the outlet and the free surface plus the horizontal motion if the outlet moves, and the jet area at the impact `A_i = Q / U_i` (so the radius at impact follows from Q and U, it is not an independent constant). Examine the previously reported peak `Q = 2.28e-6 m3/s`: is it a numerical event-rate spike (events binned by 30 ms ticks versus 50 ms field steps, quantisation) or a physical flow? The pour is defined by `pourFraction` and `pouredMl` in `lib/tubePourImpact.ts` (a fixed 1 mL portion delivered in the 0.35 to 0.76 part of a 1.7 s pour, about 0.7 s): compute the instantaneous flow from that delivered-volume curve, compare with the event-rate series at 30 ms and 50 ms bins, and report the real peak and mean. State the earlier "0.8e-6 m3/s" assumption (1 mL over 1.25 s) as wrong or right, with numbers. Provide the series Q(t), U_lip(t), U_impact(t), A_impact(t), F(t) for the real pour as a CSV and as a tested function. The impact point stays 6.5 mm from the axis (do not change the pouring geometry); report how far that is from the inner wall in the corrected geometry (from `vesselGeometry.innerRadius`).
