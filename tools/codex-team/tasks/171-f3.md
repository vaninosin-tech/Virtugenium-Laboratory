## Customer decision (binding)

At 14 s of the last real-game run the budgets were fine 0.690, aggregates 0.219, sediment 0.0515 of 0.960 formed, but the resolved nodes are microscopic (`<0.5 mm` bins hold nearly all of the 260-node cap; only a handful exceed 0.5 mm) and a large part of the aggregate mass (0.12 of 0.19 at 20 s in the LOW replay) sits in conserved sub-resolution "seeds" that are advected like the fine carrier and have no size-dependent slip. The task is to fix the aggregate REPRESENTATION and its connection to physical mass, not to spawn thousands of visible particles and not to retune blindly. Do not restart the pouring, chemistry, vessel geometry or fine-suspension systems. `__silverJetModel` and `__silverSubgrid` stay OFF. ## What to build
1. **Sectional (population-balance) representation.** Replace the "single node cap plus seeds advected like fine carrier" by size classes: material that is too small for an individual 3D object lives in sections (geometric size bins from the primary particle to a few mm; each section per spatial cell or per coarse cell: conserved mass, number or characteristic size and porosity from the fractal relation, with the mass and the number density needed for collision and aggregation), each section with its OWN advection by the resolved flow plus its own size-dependent slip velocity (porous-sphere drag with the Reynolds correction, effective density from the solid fraction), so unresolved material settles according to its size and density, is carried by the flow, and can reach the bottom and enter the sediment; transfers between sections through the same collision kernels (Brownian and shear, with the sticking probability) conserve mass exactly (Smoluchowski discretisation with mass conservation, for example the fixed-pivot or cell-average scheme) and never create material. Only sufficiently large material is promoted to an individually represented floc. State the section scheme, the number of sections, its cost per cell (the grid is 22 x 44 x 22 on HIGH and 12 x 24 x 12 on LOW), and test mass conservation.
2. **Growth.** Particles and small aggregates combine through the existing collision and attachment model; growth transfers mass consistently; aggregate radius is derived from the represented mass and the fractal relation, never enlarged independently of mass and porosity; no age-based scaling.
3. **Promotion to visible flocs.** A documented criterion based on physical size, resolved spatial structure and rendering requirement (for example: a section population in a cell whose characteristic radius exceeds a fraction of the cell size, or a coherent mass cluster above a threshold mass, so that the object is at least about half a grid cell and the renderer can show it) promotes material into an individually represented floc (a `FlocSystem` node cluster with links, so it has connected, non-spherical, deformable structure). Promotion removes the same mass from the section populations; a visible floc never exists without its mass budget; demotion/erosion returns mass to the sections.
4. **Node-limit behaviour.** When the active-node budget is reached: no freezing of additional aggregate mass, no blocking of its motion or settling, no discarding, no direct dump into the bottom sediment, no global stop of aggregation. Use the section populations as the overflow (they keep moving and settling and can be promoted later when slots free up), merging of nearby flocs, and a level-of-detail rule. Measure the dependence of the result on the node budget (compare 130, 260, and higher diagnostic budgets such as 520 and 1040 when the performance permits: CPU ms per step, mass in each representation, the size distribution of the visible flocs, the sediment curve) and show that the physics is NOT budget-dependent beyond a stated tolerance: a larger budget alone is not the solution.
5. **Loose connected structure.** Flocs must be larger than single grains, with irregular internal structure: growth by aggregation, connected non-spherical shapes (links), advection with the resolved flow, deformation and rotation (the existing plastic links and relaxation), separation where the resolved stress justifies it (the `mu G` criterion against a strength that decreases with size; report whether the breakage switch should be on), settling by effective size, density and drag. No rigid prefabricated shapes. Report the largest floc dimensions over time (bounding box, radius of gyration, number of nodes) and the floc size distribution.
6. **Fine suspension preserved.** The fine field keeps producing the milkiness: no transfer of most fine material just to make objects visible, no reserved fixed percentage; only the modeled population transfers decide. With 0.1 M the collision rate is much higher than at 0.01 M: report the resulting fine, section, floc and sediment masses and whether the fine suspension keeps its milkiness (give the fine-suspension optical depth across the tube diameter with `kappa = 8` of `SILVER_FINE_OPTICS`).
7. **Sedimentation of all populations.** All relevant populations (resolved flocs and unresolved sections) settle with their own slip velocities; no permanent suspension because of a representation limit; material at the bottom enters the sediment state; sediment bulk volume from a packing model (porosity of loose precipitate, compaction under load, never by age), checked against `tubeVessel.areaAtHeight` and the liquid volume; report the deposited mass and bulk height in mm for several porosities, with the actual 0.1 M mass (0.1 mmol AgCl = 14.3 mg: compact volume 2.6e-9 m3; at 90 percent porosity bulk 2.6e-8 m3 = 0.026 mL; in a 7 mm tube that is about 0.7 mm of uniform fill: say what the physical budget predicts instead of fabricating more solid: if the deposit is genuinely thin, report it as such).
8. **Diagnostics over time** (50 ms CSV and tables at 1, 3, 6, 9, 14, 20 s, LOW and HIGH): fine mass; total aggregate-population mass; individually represented (visible) aggregate mass; mass in each unresolved size class; sediment mass; number of active visual nodes; aggregate size distribution (counts and mass by size bins); largest aggregate dimensions; all transfer rates and cumulative transfers (fine to section, section to section, section to floc, floc to section/fine (erosion), floc and section to sediment). Do not report only the total aggregate mass: we must understand why existing aggregate mass does or does not produce large visible flocs.
